## A. The enacted Saver's Match (SECURE 2.0 §103 / IRC §6433) and its political economy

### What it is

Section 103 of the SECURE 2.0 Act added §6433 to the Internal Revenue Code, replacing the nonrefundable Saver's Credit (IRC §25B) with a federal **Saver's Match** for taxable years beginning after December 31, 2026 — i.e., first effective for tax year 2027 (CRS, In Focus IF11159, updated September 4, 2025, https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11159; downloaded copy `crs-if11159-savers-credit-savers-match-2025.pdf`). **[verified 2026-06-10]**

Core parameters **[verified 2026-06-10** against CRS IF11159, IRS Notice 2024-65, and Mercer's implementation summary**]**:

- **Match rate and cap.** 50 percent of up to $2,000 of qualified retirement savings contributions per individual — a maximum of $1,000 per person per year.
- **Delivery.** Unlike the Saver's Credit, the match is a refundable credit **paid by Treasury as a deposit into a retirement account designated by the individual** — a 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b), or traditional (non-Roth) IRA. It does not reduce tax liability; it arrives as new money in the account.
- **Phaseouts (modified AGI).** The 50 percent rate phases down linearly to zero over: $20,500–$35,500 (single/married filing separately), $30,750–$53,250 (head of household), $41,000–$71,000 (married filing jointly). The MAGI thresholds are indexed for inflation after 2027; the $2,000 contribution base is **not** indexed (IRS Notice 2024-65, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-24-65.pdf, in repo at `Infrastructure/references/literature/papers/IRS-notice-2024-65_savers-match-implementation.pdf`).
- **Distribution offset.** §6433(d)(2) reduces qualified contributions (not below zero) by distributions taken during a testing period covering the taxable year and the two preceding years.
- **Timing.** The match must be deposited "as soon as practicable" after the individual files a tax return claiming it; first deposits are therefore expected in 2028 for TY2027 contributions (Mercer, "Treasury and IRS start warming up for SECURE 2.0's saver's match," November 5, 2024, https://www.mercer.com/insights/law-and-policy/treasury-and-irs-start-warming-up-for-secure-2-0-s-savers-match/). **[verified 2026-06-10]**

### How it passed

- SECURE 2.0 was enacted as **Division T of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 (P.L. 117-328)**, signed December 29, 2022. **[verified 2026-06-10** — the executive order itself cites "the bipartisan SECURE 2.0 Act (Public Law 117-328, Division T)"**]**
- The omnibus passed the Senate 68–29 (December 22, 2022) and the House 225–201 (December 23, 2022). The retirement title consolidated three bipartisan bills: the House's Securing a Strong Retirement Act (H.R. 2954, passed 414–5 in March 2022, led by Ways and Means Chairman Richard Neal (D-MA) and Ranking Member Kevin Brady (R-TX)); the Senate Finance Committee's EARN Act (reported unanimously, 28–0; see S. Rept. 117-142); and the Senate HELP Committee's RISE & SHINE Act. The Saver's Match provision descends from the long-running Cardin–Portman retirement-security partnership (Sens. Ben Cardin (D-MD) and Rob Portman (R-OH)), whose EARN Act contained the match conversion. **[background** — vote totals and bill lineage from general knowledge of the record; S. Rept. 117-142 existence confirmed by search 2026-06-10, https://www.congress.gov/congressional-report/117th-congress/senate-report/142/1**]**
- **Revenue score.** The Joint Committee on Taxation's estimate of SECURE 2.0 revenue provisions is JCX-21-22 (JCT, 2022), already in the repository at `Infrastructure/references/literature/papers/x-21-22_jct_jcx-21-22.pdf`. Panelists should pull the §103 line from that document directly rather than from this brief.

### Implementation status as of June 2026

- **Notice 2024-65 (September 2024).** Treasury/IRS issued a request for comments on all aspects of SECURE 2.0 §§103–104, with comments due November 4, 2024 (IRS, 2024, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-24-65.pdf; in repo). The notice flagged the central open design questions: which accounts can receive the match for people who have none; that **plans are not required to accept Saver's Match deposits**; aggregation vs. per-participant transmission of deposits; Form 5500 reporting; treatment of erroneous contributions; the recovery tax on early distributions of match dollars; and required participant notices (Mercer, 2024, URL above). **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **No proposed regulations yet.** Web searches on 2026-06-10 found **no proposed Treasury regulations or further substantive IRS notices under §6433** beyond Notice 2024-65. The IRS's initial 2025–2026 Priority Guidance Plan was downloaded for reference (`irs-priority-guidance-plan-2025-2026.pdf`, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-counsel/2025-2026-initial-pgp.pdf); whether it lists a §6433 guidance project was not independently confirmed (text extraction was unavailable in this environment). **[verified absence-of-finding, 2026-06-10]**
- **IRSAC recommendations (January 2026).** The IRS Advisory Council urged the IRS to prioritize Saver's Match implementation: permit participant self-certification of eligibility, do not force plans to accept match deposits, exclude match dollars from hardship-withdrawal and mandatory-cashout rules, publish model plan amendments, and build an eligibility lookup tool. IRSAC's stated core risk: "The success of the Saver's Match program depends upon convincing retirement plan and IRA administrators to accept the contributions" (ASPPA, January 21, 2026, https://www.asppa-net.org/news/2026/1/prioritize-the-savers-match-advisory-council-tells-irs/). **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **Promotion report deadline.** SECURE 2.0 §104 requires Treasury to report to Congress by **July 1, 2026** on its plans to promote the Saver's Match (Mercer, 2024). The April 2026 executive order (Section B below) directs TrumpIRA.gov to be designed "in accordance with section 104(a) of the SECURE 2.0 Act." **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **No statutory amendments.** The 2025 budget-reconciliation law ("One Big Beautiful Bill") did not amend §6433 or other retirement-plan provisions (ASPPA, May 2025, https://www.asppa-net.org/news/2025/5/reconciliation-bill-spares-retirement-plans-in-a-big-win-for-savers/). Searches found no other enacted amendments to §6433 through 2026-06-10. **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **The "access gap" problem.** The match is payable only into an existing qualifying account, and neither §6433 nor current guidance creates an account for eligible workers who lack one. Commentators across the industry (Mercer; IRSAC; Bloomberg Law, "Trump's Retirement Account Push Meets Low-Income Savers' Reality," 2026) identify this — plus voluntary plan/IRA acceptance of deposits and recordkeeper system readiness — as the binding implementation constraints going into 2027. **[verified 2026-06-10]**

## B. The Trump executive order on retirement access and TrumpIRA.gov

**This item is fully verified.** The executive order exists, the website exists, and both were captured on 2026-06-10.

### The order

**"Promoting Retirement-Savings Access for American Workers by Establishing TrumpIRA.gov," signed April 30, 2026** (White House, 2026, https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/04/promoting-retirement-savings-access-for-american-workers-by-establishing-trumpira-gov/; full HTML captured as `trumpira-eo-2026-04-30-whitehouse.html`). No EO number appears on the White House page; the Federal Register number was not verified for this brief. Press coverage reports the order operationalizes a proposal President Trump introduced in his February 2026 State of the Union address (CNN, April 30, 2026, https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/30/economy/trump-executive-order-new-retirement-plan-proposal). **[verified 2026-06-10]**

What the order directs, by section **[verified against the captured EO text]**:

- **Sec. 1 (Policy).** Frames the access gap (workers at small businesses, part-time workers, independent contractors, the self-employed) and states the policy of giving these workers "access to the same type of retirement-savings opportunities offered to every Federal worker," explicitly invoking the TSP as the benchmark and the SECURE 2.0 Saver's Match as the matching vehicle.
- **Sec. 2 (TrumpIRA.gov).** The **Secretary of the Treasury** must, **by January 1, 2027**, establish TrumpIRA.gov — a website listing private-sector financial institutions that (i) offer IRAs under 26 U.S.C. §408, (ii) **accept Federal Saver's Match contributions under §6433(e)(2)(C)**, and (iii) meet quality criteria: a menu including life-cycle/target-date or balanced funds or principal-protection funds; **overall net expense ratios capped at 0.15 percent**; and **no minimum-contribution or minimum-balance requirements**. The site must let users filter and compare IRAs on cost and quality and must explain Saver's Match eligibility, per SECURE 2.0 §104(a).
- **Sec. 3 (Saver's Match implementation).** Treasury shall "take all necessary steps" to ensure qualifying individuals — including those who open IRAs through TrumpIRA.gov — receive the match, and to **encourage financial institutions to accept Saver's Match deposits**.
- **Sec. 4 (Charitable contributions).** Treasury/IRS shall issue guidance on tax-exempt organizations contributing to workers' IRAs without jeopardizing exempt status.
- **Sec. 5 (Worker protection).** Treasury and the **Department of Labor** shall issue regulations/exemptions/guidance so listed IRAs protect workers, maintain transparency, and prevent prohibited transactions (26 U.S.C. §4975).
- **Sec. 6 (Legislative recommendations).** Treasury, with the NEC, shall prepare recommendations to **codify** the policy — low fees, Saver's Match or other matching eligibility, diversified index-based options, automatic portfolio choices, and portability for workers without employer plans.

The accompanying fact sheet cites roughly **41 million workers aged 18–65** without access to employer-sponsored plans and a compounding illustration in which a 25-year-old contributing about $165/month plus the $1,000 annual match reaches roughly $465,000 by 65 at 6 percent returns (White House Fact Sheet, April 30, 2026; captured as `whitehouse-fact-sheet-trumpira-2026-04-30.html` — note the page's URL slug is mislabeled on whitehouse.gov but the content is the TrumpIRA fact sheet). **[verified 2026-06-10]**

### TrumpIRA.gov as of June 2026

The site is **live as a pre-launch informational page** operated by the Department of the Treasury (page last published June 5, 2026; captured 2026-06-10 as `trumpira-gov-capture-2026-06-10.html`). It describes three functions effective at the January 1, 2027 launch: (1) **"The Match"** — explaining the $1,000 Saver's Match; (2) **"The Marketplace"** — filter-and-compare listings of vetted private-sector IRAs by cost, quality, and investment options; (3) **"The People"** — targeting the 41 million workers without employer plans. It currently offers only an email sign-up. **It is a comparison/information portal, not an enrollment system or account provider** — users open IRAs directly with listed private institutions. **[verified 2026-06-10]**

### How it does and does not address the access gap

- **What it does:** It attacks the *deposit-destination* problem from the IRA side — listed providers must accept §6433(e)(2)(C) match deposits, carry sub-0.15 percent fees, and impose no minimums — and directs Treasury to push institutions to accept match deposits generally. It supplies the §104 public-awareness function. **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **What it does not do:** It creates **no accounts, no auto-enrollment, no default contribution, no new money, and no mandate**. Saving remains opt-in through tax filing and voluntary IRA opening; the match parameters remain those of §6433. An executive order cannot change the statute. The order's own Sec. 6 concedes this by directing legislative recommendations for codification. **[verified 2026-06-10]**

### Reactions

- **EIG** (April 30, 2026): president John Lettieri called the order "an important first step in addressing the fundamental flaw in the U.S. retirement system," citing 54 million workers without employer-plan access and ~80 percent of bottom-decile workers uncovered; EIG urged Congress to codify and extend the order with **automatic enrollment** and expanded matching, and reiterated support for the RSAA (EIG, 2026, https://eig.org/statement-on-retirement-eo/). EIG-affiliated estimates cited in press coverage put ~26 million workers gaining full or partial practical access to the match via the order (HR Brew, May 5, 2026, https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/2026/05/05/trump-order-retirement-savings-match). **Panel note: EIG is the home institution of this repository; treat its estimates accordingly.** **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **Aspen Institute FSP** (KC Boas): impact hinges on "whether participation is effectively driven at scale through mechanisms like auto-enrollment" (HR Brew, 2026). **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **AEI** (Mark J. Warshawsky, May 5, 2026, https://www.aei.org/economics/trumpira-gov/): questions the need for a government platform given private rollover/comparison infrastructure and state auto-IRAs ($2.8 billion across 1.2 million accounts); notes only 5.7 percent of taxpayers claimed the predecessor Saver's Credit (average $191) and that few target-date funds (~6.4 percent) meet the 0.15 percent fee cap; calls it a missed opportunity relative to Social Security reform. **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **ERISA bar/industry** (Groom Law Group, 2026, https://www.groom.com/resources/executive-order-directs-agencies-to-promote-access-to-individual-retirement-accounts/; J.P. Morgan Asset Management, 2026; 401(k) Specialist roundup, 2026): generally treat the order as a promotion-and-listing exercise within existing law, with the operative questions being Treasury's listing criteria and institutions' willingness to accept match deposits. **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- CNBC reported (May 1, 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/01/legislative-action-could-increase-us-retirement-wealth.html) research finding that the order **combined with congressional action** could raise U.S. retirement wealth by up to 77 percent; the underlying study could not be retrieved (HTTP 403), so the attribution and modeling details are **unverified** beyond the headline. **[partially verified]**

### Design (verified against S.1526 bill text)

- **American Worker Retirement Plan.** A federal program of portable, individually owned retirement accounts for "qualifying workers" — private-sector workers without an employer plan, plus independent/gig workers.
- **Auto-enrollment at 3 percent** of compensation, with opt-out and the ability to change rates.
- **Federal match delivered as a refundable tax credit deposited into the account** (treated as an overpayment, direct-deposited to the participant's account): **100 percent on contributions up to 3 percent of gross income, 50 percent on contributions between 3 and 5 percent** (i.e., up to a 4 percent match), **plus a 1 percent automatic federal contribution** for all eligible participants — a maximum 5 percent-of-income federal contribution. Note the match key is the worker's **contribution rate**, not a dollar schedule.
- **Phaseout at median income.** The credit limit falls by $75 per $1,000 of gross income above the threshold: 100 percent of median personal income (single), 150 percent (head of household), 200 percent (joint).
- **TSP-like investment menu:** Government Securities, Fixed-Income, Common Stock Index, Small-Cap Index, International Index, and Life-Cycle target-date funds, under an independent board.
- **Portability:** balances are worker-owned, inheritable, and transferable to other qualified plans.

**[verified 2026-06-10** against the bill text via govinfo**]**

### Political economy

**Supporters.** The 2025 reintroduction lists AARP, the Charles Schwab Corporation, the **Economic Innovation Group**, and the Chamber of Progress as endorsers (Hickenlooper press release, 2025). EIG has been among the most prominent advocates since 2022 — **panelists should weigh home-team bias when reading EIG-sourced framing in this repository**. **[verified 2026-06-10]**

**Opponents and critiques.**
- The **American Retirement Association/ASPPA** argues a government plan with subsidized matching would lead employers — especially small ones — to drop 401(k)s, undermining the employer system; industry groups contend SECURE 2.0, state auto-IRAs, and auto-enrollment are already closing the gap (ASPPA, 2025; 401(k) Specialist, "Retirement Savings for Americans Act: Pros and Cons," 2025, https://401kspecialistmag.com/controversial-retirement-bill-reaches-congress-again/). **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- **Morningstar Center for Retirement & Policy Studies** (Spencer Look and Jack VanDerhei, November 2024, https://www.morningstar.com/retirement/will-retirement-savings-americans-act-save-retirement; also SSRN 5285518): simulations find the RSAA expands access but **lowers projected retirement wealth ~20 percent for Gen Z and ~12 percent for millennials** in baseline scenarios, because the 3 percent federal default and crowd-out of higher-contribution private DC plans reduce net saving for anyone with substantial future DC participation; workers with little or no future DC coverage gain. **[verified 2026-06-10]**
- Conservative/market critics characterize a federally run plan as a step toward government dominance of retirement saving and question creating a costly program while Social Security is unaddressed (401(k) Specialist, 2025; AEI commentary). A widely cited RSAA-specific critique by **Andrew Biggs** was searched for but not specifically located; AEI's retirement skeptics (Biggs on the "retirement crisis" framing generally; Warshawsky on TrumpIRA.gov) supply the closest verified critiques. **[verified absence-of-finding, 2026-06-10]**
- **Why it has not advanced:** no committee action in three Congresses; jurisdictional weight sits with Senate Finance and House Ways and Means; the employer-plan industry (ARA, ICI-aligned commenters) opposes; and the fiscal cost of a 5 percent-of-payroll federal contribution program has no official score to anchor negotiation. **[verified status; interpretive synthesis]**

**Scoring.** **No JCT or CBO score of the RSAA was located** as of 2026-06-10. Contrary to the panel brief's working assumption, **searches found no Penn Wharton Budget Model analysis of the RSAA**; PWBM's closest work is an illustrative automatic retirement-savings policy for ~56 million low-income workers funded by capping the 401(k) exclusion (PWBM, July 2024, https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/issues/2024/7/16/automatic-retirement-savings-plans-for-low-income-households; downloaded). The RSAA-specific quantitative analyses located are **RAND**, "Impacts of the Retirement Savings for Americans Act: A Preliminary Analysis Using the RAND Budget Model" (RRA2614-3, 2024, https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA2614-3.html; PDF downloaded) and the **Morningstar** simulation above. **[verified 2026-06-10]**

## D. Where the repository's hybrid proposal sits

The hybrid proposal (`Infrastructure/specs/2026-05-28_universal-account-savers-match-hybrid-proposal.md`) pairs a universal default account (auto-enrollment at 3 percent; routing to employer plan where one exists, otherwise to a federal TSP-like account) with a redesigned Saver's Match: a **MAGI-linear match schedule with a 200 percent floor at zero MAGI**, declining to 50 percent at pivots of $32,879 (single) / $49,319 (HoH) / $65,758 (MFJ) and to zero at four-thirds of the pivot; a **$1,000 per-individual cap**; **46.07 million eligible workers**; **$14.9 billion** headline annual cost (59.8 percent take-up), with a $24.9 billion full-participation ceiling. Facts for the panel's comparison:

**Versus enacted §6433 (current law).**
- The proposal is a **parameter redesign plus an access fix**: it keeps §6433's MAGI eligibility concept, per-individual $1,000 cap, and refundable deposit delivery, but replaces the 50 percent flat-rate-with-cliff-phaseout with a linear schedule whose subsidy rate rises to 200 percent at the bottom, and replaces static dollar thresholds with income-distribution-anchored pivots.
- It directly answers the two implementation gaps that dominate the 2024–2026 guidance record (Notice 2024-65; IRSAC 2026): *where the match lands* for workers without accounts (the universal default account) and *who contributes* (the 3 percent default), neither of which the statute or the EO provides.
- Requires statute: both the match-schedule change and the account/auto-enrollment architecture are beyond Treasury's §6433 authority.

**Versus the EO/TrumpIRA.gov.**
- Same diagnosis (the access gap; TSP as benchmark; low fees), different instrument. The EO is **executive action within existing law**: information, listing criteria, and moral suasion on institutions to accept match deposits — no accounts, no defaults, no parameter changes. The hybrid is **statutory**: automatic accounts, automatic contributions, and a more generous, differently shaped match.
- The EO's Sec. 6 legislative-recommendations directive and EIG's codify-and-extend response define the live legislative opening the hybrid would compete in.
- Overlap to probe: both rely on private or federal account infrastructure accepting Treasury match deposits under §6433(e)(2)(C)-type mechanics; the EO's 0.15 percent fee cap and no-minimums criteria are an executive analog of the hybrid's TSP-like account standards.

**Versus the RSAA.**
- Shared architecture: universal federal account, 3 percent auto-enrollment default, TSP-like menu, portability, refundable-credit-into-account match delivery.
- Key design difference: **RSAA keys the match to the worker's contribution rate** (100 percent of first 3 percent of income + 50 percent of next 2 percent, plus a 1 percent automatic contribution, phasing out at median income); **the hybrid keys the match rate to MAGI** (200 percent at zero MAGI, linear to zero past the pivot) with a flat $1,000 dollar cap. The hybrid therefore concentrates subsidy dollars on the lowest earners at a higher effective rate (up to 200 percent vs. RSAA's 100 percent) and caps fiscal exposure per person in dollars rather than as a share of income; RSAA's federal cost scales with income up to the cap and includes a non-contingent 1 percent contribution the hybrid omits.
- The hybrid's eligibility frontier (zero match at $43,839/$65,758/$87,678) sits below RSAA's median-income-anchored phaseout starting points for most filing groups; both index eligibility to the income distribution rather than fixed dollars, unlike §6433 (CPI-indexed from 2028 off static 2027 thresholds).
- Both face the same critiques on record against the RSAA — crowd-out of employer plans (ARA; Morningstar's −12 to −20 percent wealth simulations), "nationalization" objections, and fiscal cost without an official score — and the hybrid's $14.9 billion headline (repo simulation, SIPP 2024 projected to TY2027) has no external JCT/CBO/RAND counterpart. Employer-side responses are explicitly unmodeled in the repo's simulation.
