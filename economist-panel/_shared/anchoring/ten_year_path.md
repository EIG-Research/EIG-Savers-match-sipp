# Corrected Ten-Year Outlay Path (FY2027–FY2036)

**Date:** 2026-06-11. Replaces the first-round figures in Okafor's assessment ($151.5B / $202.1B / $252.6B), which were computed on the old base and an old (median/wage-indexed) growth assumption. Computed by `temp/deck_build/ten_year_path.R`.

**Stated assumptions (the panel may contest these — this is the open methodological choice, not a settled number):**
- FY2027 base costs are the current re-anchored rungs: $14.15B / $19.09B / $23.87B.
- **C-CPI-U central path: ~2.7%/yr net** — chained-CPI cap/band indexation (~2.3%, CBO-grounded) plus net workforce growth (~0.4%), with the eligible share held roughly stable by the decennial re-anchor over the window.
- **Wage-indexed alternative: ~3.9%/yr** — what the old median-indexed design implied; shown only to isolate the indexing effect.
- "Flat" = no-growth convention, comparable to the first-round figures' apparent convention; used to isolate the base effect.

| Rung (FY2027 base) | 10-yr, flat | 10-yr, C-CPI-U (≈2.7%) | 10-yr, wage-indexed (≈3.9%) | Year-10 annual (C-CPI-U) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative — SIPP-observed ~59% ($14.15B) | $141.5B | **$160.0B** | $169.1B | $18.0B |
| **Headline — auto-enroll 80% ($19.09B)** | $190.9B | **$215.8B** | $228.1B | $24.3B |
| Ceiling — full participation ($23.87B) | $238.7B | **$269.9B** | $285.3B | $30.3B |

**Two effects, separated:**
- **Base effect** — the re-anchoring lowers the starting cost, so the 10-year sum is ~6 percent below the first-round figures on a like-for-like (flat) convention ($190.9B vs. $202.1B for the 80% rung).
- **Indexing effect** — the D1 switch from median/wage-indexing to C-CPI-U with a decennial re-anchor **slows the trajectory**: the 10-year growth premium drops from ~19.5% (wage-indexed) to ~13% (C-CPI-U), about 6 percentage points less cumulative growth. The C-CPI-U choice therefore lowers the long-run path relative to the old design, even though both grow.

**Bottom line for external use:** under the brief's 80 percent auto-enrollment headline, the program runs about **$216 billion over its first ten years** (range $191–228B depending on the indexing assumption), reaching roughly $24 billion in annual cost by year ten.
