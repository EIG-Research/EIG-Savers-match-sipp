## 1. Disposition check

The changelog reads my three recommendations correctly. **Recommendation 2 (CPI-index the pivots; reframe the floor) — adopted**, and in a stronger form than I asked for: an IRS administrative anchor, C-CPI-U indexing (the Code's own post-TCJA convention), a decennial re-anchor, and the "$500 unlocks $1,000" framing in the brief. ## 2. The decline: D2 and the federal account

My first-round objection was never that the federal account is bad policy. It was that the account is the one element with three documented institutional opponents (ICI, ARA, IRI), a failed precedent (MyRA, 30,000 participants, closed 2017), and no committee champion — and that the RSAA's three-Congress death in Senate Finance is the controlled experiment. D2's rationale — worker-level portability avoids churn, cash-out, and vesting erosion — is a *policy* answer to a *coalition* objection. It does not answer the objection; it concedes it and overrides it, on the record ("the political cost is accepted knowingly," D2 downstream-impact note).

Is that an informed bet or an unforced error? The honest answer is: an informed bet, executed without a hedge. The routing arithmetic on the new model is essentially unchanged from the figure I flagged in round one (57.9 percent then, **58.4 percent now**): 25.98M of 44.47M eligibles default to the Treasury-run account, carrying $13.95B of the $23.87B full-participation dollars (58.5 percent) at a mean match rate of 84.7pp versus 65.8pp on the employer route (my recomputation). The majority of the program's beneficiaries and dollars still live in the contested channel, and the account now visibly *is* the RSAA's American Worker Retirement Plan — which means the proposal inherits the RSAA's enemies by name, not just by analogy. Yes, this re-runs the MyRA/RSAA fight, with one aggravation: the 84.7pp average subsidy rate on the federal route hands ARA a sharper version of its crowd-out argument than the RSAA ever did.

What would change the stakeholder map without surrendering portability — because the portability rationale is genuinely strong, and I concede that worker-level accounts are the right substrate for it:

- **Private administration of the federal account.** Every operating state auto-IRA runs on a private program manager. A statutory requirement that Treasury procure recordkeeping competitively (or auction the default-provider role) converts recordkeepers and fintech from opponents into bidders. The account survives as architecture; the government-administrator monopoly, which is what ICI actually opposes, does not.
- **Extend substitution to unattached workers.** The spec already lets *employers* substitute compliant state or private auto-IRAs. Workers with no employer — the self-employed 4.26M and the no-plan W-2 population — get no such option; they default federal, full stop. Letting an EO-marketplace-listed IRA (sub-0.15 percent fees, no minimums, match-accepting) serve as a qualifying default destination gives the TrumpIRA.gov coalition a stake in passage and lets the federal account be framed as the backstop where no listed provider will take small-dollar accounts.
- **Brand the account as the codification of Section 6.** The EO's own legislative-recommendations directive asks Congress for portability and match eligibility for workers without employer plans. A design that presents the federal account as finishing the administration's portal — rather than as the RSAA resurrected — at least scrambles the partisan coding that killed MyRA.

With one or more of these, my objection substantially dissolves. Without them, it stands: the document has knowingly priced the cost but bought nothing with it.

## 3. Fresh eyes

**The 200 percent floor (F1).** The model cannot adjudicate the floor (it assumes match-invariant participation), so the bet is legitimately behavioral. My lane is the optics, and the adopted reframing covers most of it. One political caution on S2: a *legislated randomized match arm* inside a tax credit invites a horizontal-equity attack ("the IRS is flipping coins with your match") that committee staff dislike. A staged-escalation evaluation (launch at 200 percent, sunset to 100 percent absent demonstrated participation effects, with the evaluation written into the statute) buys the same evidence with safer politics.

**The anchor population (F3).** The IRS anchor is a major political upgrade — JCT-replicable, legislator-legible. But do not sell ⅔ as "calibrated to reproduce prior coverage within 3–4 percent"; a hostile staffer reads that as reverse-engineering. The schedule has a cleaner native description: with the Single endpoint at 1.067× the IRS single-filer median, **every below-median single filer receives a positive match**. Lead with that; it is true, checkable, and defensible on its own terms.

**Which number leads (F4).** The conservative one. In a scoring vacuum — there is still no JCT/CBO score, the same vacuum that helped stall the RSAA — whatever number the proposal publishes becomes the anchor, and opponents will quote the largest number on the page regardless. Foregrounding $19.09B (and ~$216B/ten years) in Hill-facing material supplies the attack-ad number voluntarily. Analytically the 80 percent case is the better central estimate; politically, external material should lead with **$14.15B at the SIPP-observed 59.0 percent (~$160B/ten years), the full ladder disclosed, and the zero-losers fact beside it** — my refreshed losers' map confirms that among the 33.08M current-law eligibles there remain **zero** strict rate losers and zero dollar losers at a $2,000 contribution under the new pivots (minimum hybrid-minus-current-law gap: +34.8pp). That Pareto property over the existing beneficiary file is the proposal's single best political asset; the 14.56M MFJ bloc (32.7 percent of eligibles, including 4.19M above the current-law $71,000 cutoff) remains the swing-district sweetener.

**Debt financing (F5).** No-pay-for is not viable. §103 passed at $9.3B/ten years with half the cost pushed outside the window; this proposal is roughly twenty times that on its own ten-year math. The RAND offset is real but lands 30–45 years out — outside any budget window JCT will score. A design document may legitimately stop at "debt-financed," but it should name the pay-for menu it could live with (Okafor's DC-exclusion envelope is the obvious candidate) before a committee names one it can't.

**New elements.** The **decennial re-anchor** is well-designed politics: automatic by statute (no recurring expansion vote that, like the minimum wage, would simply never happen), with C-CPI-U discipline between — but flag that each re-anchor year is a scheduled JCT re-score event and a predictable "automatic benefit expansion" headline; the draft should say the step is anticipated and bounded. The **six-month forfeiture** is a favorable trade versus §6433(d)(2) — "keep it six months and it's yours" is sellable — though Treasury clawing back $400 from a home-health aide's account is an ombudsman story waiting to happen; a de-minimis tolerance is cheap insurance. **Prior-year MAGI** is the best administrability sell in the document (match known at contribution, no reconciliation, FAFSA prior-prior-year precedent); specify the new-filer default rule before drafting. On the open Tier-2 take-up items: anchor the W-2 default-participation assumption to state auto-IRA opt-out evidence (those programs run *unmatched*, so they bound the hybrid from below), assume materially lower self-employed take-up despite the D10 rails, and remember the politics cares less about the right number than about which number you publish first.

## 4. Updated verdict

**Support with modifications — unchanged from round one, but the modification has narrowed.** In round one I asked for amputation of the federal-account default; the adopted D1 indexing, the prior-year-MAGI basis, and the preserved zero-losers property have strengthened the surrounding package enough that I now ask for insulation rather than amputation. Conditions, in priority order:

1. **Statutorily insulate the federal account**: competitively procured private administration and/or marketplace-and-state substitution rights for unattached workers, so the channel that carries 58.4 percent of beneficiaries converts at least part of its organized opposition into vendors.
2. **Lead external material with the conservative rung** ($14.15B; ~$160B/ten years) and the zero-losers fact; keep the 80 percent case as a labeled scenario, and seek an official score early.
3. **Name a pay-for menu** — debt-financed-with-a-RAND-footnote will not survive Finance Committee contact.
